# Modash's exact Instagram engagement rate methodology and rationale

Research date: 23 November 2026. All "primary" sources below are Modash-owned pages (help.modash.io, modash.io). Third-party summaries are explicitly labeled. **Important: Modash's own current pages conflict on the time window (roughly 2 vs roughly 3 months) and on the TikTok/YouTube formula. Both variants are documented below rather than silently reconciled.**

## 1. Exact published formula, time window, and data source

### Takeaway
Modash's current Instagram engagement rate (ER) is the **median number of likes per post divided by total followers** (× 100), using recent content only. The authoritative help article dated 21 October 2026 says the window is "roughly the last three months", but the public calculator, blog, and API cheat sheet all still say about the last two months (the API sheet adds "up to around 500 posts").

### Cited Findings
- Current help article (dated October 21, 2026), verbatim: "Modash uses platform-specific formulas to calculate engagement rates because each platform works differently - and we want to give you the most accurate picture possible. **All calculations are based on roughly the last three months of content.**" Then: "**Instagram**: Median Likes ÷ Followers" — [Understanding engagement rate and how Modash calculates it](https://help.modash.io/en/articles/6542471-understanding-engagement-rate-and-how-modash-calculates-it)
- Current public calculator, verbatim: "To determine a creator's engagement rate, take the median number of likes across all their posts on a profile, divide by the total number of followers, then multiply by 100. Using this formula, you get the overall engagement across a creator's profile. **Modash uses the last two months of content**, and the median rather than the mean, so one viral post can't drag the whole figure up." Formula shown: "Median likes per post / Total followers × 100"; worked illustration: 117,000 median likes, 39M followers, 0.3% ER — [Instagram Engagement Rate Calculator](https://www.modash.io/engagement-rate-calculator)
- Modash blog (published September 17, 2026): "Here's the standard formula: Engagement rate = (Median likes per post ÷ Total followers) × 100"; per-channel list: "**Instagram:** median likes ÷ followers; **TikTok:** average likes ÷ average views; **YouTube:** average likes ÷ average views." FAQ: "Modash, for instance, uses median likes over the last 2 months for Instagram specifically to cover up any outlier viral posts" — [Influencer Engagement Rate: How to Measure It, Judge It, and Verify It](https://www.modash.io/blog/influencer-engagement-rate)
- API cheat sheet (dated October 21, 2026): "**Engagement calculations** – TikTok and YouTube: average likes divided by average views; Instagram: median likes divided by followers. Calculations typically consider the last ~2 months of content, up to around 500 posts" — [API cheat sheet: limits, filters & metrics](https://help.modash.io/en/articles/14035619-api-cheat-sheet-limits-filters-metrics)
- Data provenance, primary: "Data is collected about **every public creator profile several times a month** from places like about sections, captions & descriptions of posts, images, videos, and other public info"; "Public creator data is put through different Machine Learning models to bring you rich insights about creators & their audiences, e.g. growth rate, **engagement rate**, & number of fake followers"; Modash describes itself as an "open network creator database" listing "all influencers who post publicly"; FAQ says it includes "every public profile on Instagram, TikTok, and YouTube with over 1K followers" (350M+ profiles) — [Bringing data you trust to influencer marketing](https://www.modash.io/data)
- Refresh cadence (primary): "Very basic data (followers, name, username): at least weekly; Basic data (recent posts, engagement): roughly twice per month; Analysed data (audience, detailed metrics): about once per month; Profile reports: every 2-4 weeks; Search indices: approximately once per month" — [API cheat sheet](https://help.modash.io/en/articles/14035619-api-cheat-sheet-limits-filters-metrics). Help FAQ separately states "We recalculate engagement rate every 2-4 weeks based on the creator's recent content" — [Understanding engagement rate](https://help.modash.io/en/articles/6542471-understanding-engagement-rate-and-how-modash-calculates-it)
- Historical/archived primary versions: the help article archived 13 March 2026 and 18 July 2026 both said "roughly the **last two months** of content" and used average (not median) likes ÷ average views for TikTok/YouTube; the byline on the July capture is "Georg Kiil, February 12, 2026" — [Wayback capture 2026-03-13](https://web.archive.org/web/20260313112919/https://help.modash.io/en/articles/6542471-understanding-engagement-rate-and-how-modash-calculates-it); [Wayback capture 2026-07-18](https://web.archive.org/web/20260718172536/https://help.modash.io/en/articles/6542471-understanding-engagement-rate-and-how-modash-calculates-it). The current page keeps the Instagram formula but changes the window to ~3 months and TikTok/YouTube to "Median Likes, Comments, Shares, and Saves ÷ Views" and "Median Likes and Comments ÷ Views" — [current help article](https://help.modash.io/en/articles/6542471-understanding-engagement-rate-and-how-modash-calculates-it)
- Older primary article using a different, broader formula (2022-03-24): "add all of the engagements (likes + comments etc.) across all the influencer's posts on a particular profile, divide by the total number of followers, then multiply by 100 .... Average Organic Engagement Rate (%) = Total Engagement / Total number of followers x 100" — [How To Check Influencer Engagement Rate In 30 Seconds](https://www.modash.io/blog/how-to-check-influencer-engagement-rate). This is superseded by current pages; it shows the metric definition has changed over time.

### Inferences
- The "median likes ÷ followers" claim is well supported as Modash's current published Instagram formula across four independent primary pages; the "roughly last three months" claim is supported by only the most recent help article, and is contradicted by three other live Modash pages. A safe characterization is "recent content, documented as ~2 months in the calculator/blog/API docs and ~3 months in the current help center."
- The window moved from "two months" to "three months" in the help article between the 18 July 2026 capture and the 21 October 2026 revision; Modash did not publish an explanation for the change in the article text.
- The API cheat sheet's "up to around 500 posts" is the only published numeric cap on how many posts enter the calculation.

### Gaps
- No primary source states an exact calendar-day window, an exact post-count threshold beyond "around 500 posts", or whether the follower denominator is a single current snapshot, a post-date-matched value, or an average over the window.
- No changelog or announcement explaining the 2-month → 3-month help-text change was found.
- A search-engine indexed copy of the older free-tools URL (`https://www.modash.io/free-tools/instagram-engagement-rate-calculator`) described the metric as "median number of engagements (likes + comments etc.) ... divided by the total number of followers", but the live page at that URL now serves the same likes-only formula as the main calculator. Treat the indexed wording as stale/unverified, not as current doctrine.

## 2. Does the median include comments, saves, shares, or reach? What is the denominator?

### Takeaway
For Instagram, **no**: the numerator is median likes only and the denominator is total followers. Comments, saves, shares, views, reach, and DMs are not in this published formula; comments and average views are displayed as separate metrics.

### Cited Findings
- Help FAQ, verbatim: "**Does engagement rate include comments and shares?** For Instagram, we use median likes relative to followers. For TikTok, we measure likes, comments, shares, and saves relative to views. For YouTube, we measure likes and comments relative to views" — [Understanding engagement rate](https://help.modash.io/en/articles/6542471-understanding-engagement-rate-and-how-modash-calculates-it)
- Help body: "We use the median (not average) of likes divided by follower count" (Instagram), versus TikTok "Median Likes, Comments, Shares, and Saves ÷ Views" and YouTube "Median Likes and Comments ÷ Views" — [Understanding engagement rate](https://help.modash.io/en/articles/6542471-understanding-engagement-rate-and-how-modash-calculates-it)
- Calculator publishes "Median likes per post / Total followers × 100" while separately showing "Average likes", "Average comments", and "Average Reel plays" — [Instagram Engagement Rate Calculator](https://www.modash.io/engagement-rate-calculator)
- Blog: "**Instagram:** median likes ÷ followers"; it discusses likes, comments, saves, and shares as a qualitative "engagement hierarchy", but says "engagement rate as a single percentage doesn't really tell you much ... it compresses very different behaviors (likes, comments, saves, shares) into one number" and "doesn't take into account any interactions that happen in DMs and other non-public places" — [Influencer Engagement Rate](https://www.modash.io/blog/influencer-engagement-rate)
- Distinct metric in campaign tracking (not the Discover/profile ER): "**Average ER%** calculated based only on measurable, trackable content. Stories, creators without posts, content with hidden likes, and content with no views are excluded ... Note that **Discovery ER%** and **Campaign ER%** use different formulas (Discovery uses median engagement across recent posts, while Campaign uses per-creator averages)"; "**Average video ER%** - engagement rate calculated using actual video views as the denominator instead of follower count"; "**Average impressions ER%** - engagement rate based on estimated impressions for non-video content" — [Understanding campaign analytics](https://help.modash.io/en/articles/13717084-understanding-campaign-analytics)
- Campaign "Est. reach" for posts is separately "calculated from engagement data (likes and comments)" and is **not** used as the Instagram ER denominator — [Understanding campaign analytics](https://help.modash.io/en/articles/13717084-understanding-campaign-analytics)

### Inferences
- Modash uses at least three different ER constructions in different product contexts: Discovery/profile ER (median likes ÷ followers for Instagram), Campaign ER (per-creator averages over tracked content), and video/impressions ER (views or estimated impressions as denominator). A number labeled "ER" must be attributed to its context.
- The archived/current discrepancy applies only to TikTok/YouTube (average → median of more interaction types); the Instagram likes-only numerator has been stable in the help article since at least March 2026.

### Gaps
- The help article's phrase "median engagement across recent posts" for Discovery ER in the campaign-analytics article is not defined there in terms of which interactions it sums; the dedicated ER article and calculator specify likes only for Instagram. No further primary clarification was found.
- No source says whether Reels plays or Stories views are included in the Instagram profile ER; the calculator separately reports "Average Reel plays".

## 3. Why a median rather than a mean, and why roughly three months?

### Takeaway
Modash explains the median clearly (outlier/viral-post protection) but gives **no published rationale for the time-window length** — either the current ~3 months or the earlier ~2 months.

### Cited Findings
- Help article: "**Why median?** Because one viral post shouldn't trick you into thinking a creator consistently gets high engagement. The median filters out those spikes and shows you what *typical* performance actually looks like" — [Understanding engagement rate](https://help.modash.io/en/articles/6542471-understanding-engagement-rate-and-how-modash-calculates-it)
- Calculator: "the median rather than the mean, so one viral post can't drag the whole figure up" — [Instagram Engagement Rate Calculator](https://www.modash.io/engagement-rate-calculator)
- Blog: "**Why median for Instagram instead of average?** One viral post can drag an average wildly out of proportion ..., so the median gives you a steadier, more honest look at a creator's typical performance"; also "**1. One viral post skews everything**" and FAQ "median likes over the last 2 months for Instagram specifically to cover up any outlier viral posts" — [Influencer Engagement Rate](https://www.modash.io/blog/influencer-engagement-rate)
- Rationale for platform-specific denominators (adjacent but relevant): TikTok "For You page means a creator's content reaches way beyond their followers. Follower-based engagement would be misleading"; YouTube "algorithm drives the majority of views" — [Understanding engagement rate](https://help.modash.io/en/articles/6542471-understanding-engagement-rate-and-how-modash-calculates-it)
- No sentence in any of the fetched primary pages justifies why ~3 months (or ~2 months) was chosen; the help article simply asserts the window — [Understanding engagement rate](https://help.modash.io/en/articles/6542471-understanding-engagement-rate-and-how-modash-calculates-it); [Wayback capture 2026-07-18](https://web.archive.org/web/20260718172536/https://help.modash.io/en/articles/6542471-understanding-engagement-rate-and-how-modash-calculates-it)

### Inferences
- The median rationale is about robustness to a single viral post, i.e., measuring "typical" rather than "best-case" engagement.
- The window change from 2 to 3 months in the help article is undocumented; any interpretation (e.g., more content for sparse posters) would be speculation and is not asserted here.

### Gaps
- No primary statement explains why ~3 months was selected, why it replaced ~2 months in the help text, or how the tradeoff between recency and sample size was decided.

## 4. Accounts with few posts, irregular posting, or follower changes during the window

### Takeaway
The only published edge-case rules are: hidden likes or "haven't posted recently enough" → no ER listed; pinned posts count if inside the window; up to ~500 posts considered. No primary source specifies a minimum post count, a sparse-poster rule, or how follower changes during the window are handled.

### Cited Findings
- Help FAQ: "**Why do some creators have no engagement rate listed?** Either they've hidden their likes, or they haven't posted recently enough for us to calculate a meaningful rate. Check their posting frequency and audience quality scores instead" — [Understanding engagement rate](https://help.modash.io/en/articles/6542471-understanding-engagement-rate-and-how-modash-calculates-it)
- Help pro tip: "If a creator hides their likes, Modash can't calculate an accurate engagement rate. You'll see this noted on their profile" — [Understanding engagement rate](https://help.modash.io/en/articles/6542471-understanding-engagement-rate-and-how-modash-calculates-it)
- Help FAQ: "**Are pinned posts included in the calculation?** Yes. Pinned posts are treated as regular posts in the engagement rate calculation, as long as they fall within the content window we analyze" — [Understanding engagement rate](https://help.modash.io/en/articles/6542471-understanding-engagement-rate-and-how-modash-calculates-it)
- API cheat sheet: "Calculations typically consider the last ~2 months of content, up to around 500 posts" — [API cheat sheet](https://help.modash.io/en/articles/14035619-api-cheat-sheet-limits-filters-metrics)
- Discovery API guidance: "For **less popular profiles**, updates happen on-demand when fetched via the API or searched on Modash platform. 👉 This means some values (e.g. engagement rate) may appear outdated compared to others (e.g. follower count), which are refreshed more frequently" — [Discovery API: Best Practices & Onboarding](https://help.modash.io/en/articles/10870293-discovery-api-best-practices-onboarding-for-developers)
- Recency filters exist separately for search ("Last posted: Only show creators who've posted within the last 3, 6, 9, or 12 months") and AI search ("Posting frequency – filter by how often creators post: daily, every other day, weekly, or monthly"; "Posted within – narrow results to creators who posted within a specific timeframe") — [Find the perfect match (using search filters)](https://help.modash.io/en/articles/5688853-find-the-perfect-match-using-search-filters); [How to use AI search to find creators](https://help.modash.io/en/articles/13714842-how-to-use-ai-search-to-find-creators)
- Platform note: "Very basic data like follower count refreshes at least weekly, but there can be a short delay. Small differences are normal" — [Profile updates: How often data refreshes](https://help.modash.io/en/articles/5467354-profile-updates-how-often-data-refreshes)

### Inferences
- "Not posted recently enough" is a qualitative recency condition, not a published numeric threshold; Modash does not say what "recently enough" means in days or posts.
- Follower-count freshness is documented at the profile level (weekly), but that is not the same as documenting which follower value is used as the ER denominator when an account grows or shrinks during the content window.

### Gaps
- No primary statement of a minimum number of posts required to compute/show an ER.
- No primary rule for irregular posting (e.g., whether all posts in the window are used, or whether a fixed last-N posts rule applies).
- No primary statement on whether the denominator is the current follower count, the count at each post's date, an average over the window, or how large follower changes are treated.
- No primary statement on how deleted, archived, sponsored-only, or Reposted/cross-posted content is treated in the Instagram ER (only pinned posts are explicitly covered).

## 5. How the published tier ranges are derived, dated, caveated; platform scope; updates

### Takeaway
Modash publishes **two different-looking benchmark sets**: (a) prescriptive "good" tier ranges (Nano 5–10%, Micro 3–7%, Macro 1–3%, Mega 0.5–2%) in the help center and blog, with no stated dataset or calculation; and (b) empirical medians across "84M+ Instagram profiles" on the calculator, with no stated collection date. The ranges are presented as Instagram benchmarks (TikTok/YouTube get a single 3–6% "strong" guide), and no update cadence is published for the tables themselves.

### Cited Findings
- Help article "What's a good engagement rate?" — **Instagram**: "Nano creators (1K-10K): 5-10% - these creators have *incredible* audience relationships; Micro creators (10K-100K): 3-7% - the sweet spot for most campaigns; Macro creators (100K-1M): 1-3% - solid for broad reach plays; Mega creators (1M+): 0.5-2% - normal at this scale, still massive total engagement." **TikTok & YouTube**: "3-6% is strong across most creator sizes"; "Views-based engagement runs lower than follower-based". Caveat: "Always compare engagement rates within the same niche and follower range." No dataset, sample size, date, or derivation is given for these ranges — [Understanding engagement rate](https://help.modash.io/en/articles/6542471-understanding-engagement-rate-and-how-modash-calculates-it)
- Blog repeats the identical four Instagram tier ranges with the same descriptive comments and the 3–6% TikTok/YouTube guidance, labeled "platform-specific benchmarks for Instagram influencer marketing", with the same niche/follower-range caveat; article published April 2, 2026 — [Nano Influencer Marketing: Complete Guide](https://www.modash.io/blog/nano-influencer-marketing)
- Calculator's data-derived set: "Median engagement rate on Instagram" by band — 3.51% (1-5k), 1.00% (5-10k), 0.57% (10-50k), 0.38% (50-100k), 0.41% (100-500k), 0.45% (500k-1M), 0.51% (>1M); caption: "**Median across 84M+ Instagram profiles in the Modash database**" — [Instagram Engagement Rate Calculator](https://www.modash.io/engagement-rate-calculator)
- Calculator's percentile-style table for 1k–5k / 10k–50k / 100k–500k / 1M+: High >6.16% / >1.27% / >0.93% / >1.08%; Above average 3.85–6.16% / 0.65–1.27% / 0.46–0.93% / 0.57–1.08%; Average 3.16–3.85% / 0.49–0.65% / 0.35–0.46% / 0.45–0.57%; Below average 1.85–3.16% / 0.24–0.49% / 0.16–0.35% / 0.22–0.45%; Low <1.85% / <0.24% / <0.16% / <0.22%. Framing: "Engagement rate drops sharply once an account grows past a few thousand followers, then flattens out and ticks back up on the biggest profiles. A 3% rate on a 4K account is ordinary; the same 3% on a 500K account would be exceptional" — [Instagram Engagement Rate Calculator](https://www.modash.io/engagement-rate-calculator)
- Blog (September 17, 2026) shows a chart of "median Instagram engagement rates across follower tiers" and states "A creator with 3,000 followers posting a 2% engagement rate is actually performing below the typical range for their size. But a creator with 800,000 followers posting that same 2% has an exceptionally good engagement rate"; caveat: "The median can differ wildly by industry too" and "we don't recommend judging engagement rate against a single universal cutoff" — [Influencer Engagement Rate](https://www.modash.io/blog/influencer-engagement-rate)
- Dating caveats: help article metadata is "October 21, 2026" (HTML `dateModified`, visible byline); nano blog is dated April 2, 2026; influencer-ER blog is dated September 17, 2026. The calculator page carries a 2026 copyright but no visible "data as of" date — [help article](https://help.modash.io/en/articles/6542471-understanding-engagement-rate-and-how-modash-calculates-it); [nano blog](https://www.modash.io/blog/nano-influencer-marketing); [ER blog](https://www.modash.io/blog/influencer-engagement-rate); [calculator](https://www.modash.io/engagement-rate-calculator)
- Third-party critique (NOT Modash): "Modash's help page calls 3% to 7% good for Instagram creators with 10,000 to 100,000 followers, while Modash's own database puts the median at 0.57% for 10,000 to 50,000 and 0.38% for 50,000 to 100,000. The page's other 'good' ranges, 5% to 10% for nano and 1% to 3% for macro, also sit above the medians in the database the same company publishes"; OWM also says Modash's 84M+ medians use "the last two months of content" — [OWM, "Influencer engagement rate benchmarks"](https://www.owm.ai/learn/creator-sourcing/engagement-rate-benchmarks) (last updated November 2026, per page). This is a third-party observation, not a Modash admission.
- Refresh cadence for underlying profile stats is documented (engagement ~twice monthly; ER recalculated every 2–4 weeks), but no primary page says the benchmark tables are regenerated on that schedule — [API cheat sheet](https://help.modash.io/en/articles/14035619-api-cheat-sheet-limits-filters-metrics); [help article](https://help.modash.io/en/articles/6542471-understanding-engagement-rate-and-how-modash-calculates-it)

### Inferences
- The tier ranges in the help center/blog are presented as advisory guidance ("use these benchmarks to instantly spot standout creators"), whereas the calculator's 84M+ numbers are explicitly empirical medians/percentiles. They are not the same construct, and a third party has noted the "good" ranges sit above Modash's own medians.
- The tier ranges are Instagram-specific in both the help article and nano blog; TikTok and YouTube share one cross-platform "3-6%" guide. Because Modash says Instagram is follower-based while TikTok/YouTube are view-based, the help article itself notes views-based rates "run lower" and that rates are not directly comparable across platforms — [Understanding engagement rate](https://help.modash.io/en/articles/6542471-understanding-engagement-rate-and-how-modash-calculates-it); [Influencer Engagement Rate](https://www.modash.io/blog/influencer-engagement-rate)
- "84M+ Instagram profiles" is smaller than the 350M+/380M+ total multi-platform database, suggesting the Instagram benchmark uses an Instagram-specific subset; this is an inference from the differing published counts, not an explicit Modash statement.

### Gaps
- No primary source explains how the 5–10/3–7/1–3/0.5–2% ranges were derived, what dataset or date they reflect, or whether they are goals, industry norms, or percentile cutoffs.
- No "last updated" date or revision history is published for the benchmark tables themselves.
- No geography/niche breakdown is published for the 84M+ medians; the only caveat offered is to compare within the same niche and follower range.

## 6. Modash documentation that qualifies or critiques its own metric

### Takeaway
Modash repeatedly qualifies ER as a single, incomplete signal: it can be faked, distorted by hidden likes, compressed across unlike behaviors, blind to private interactions, and misleading if compared against a universal cutoff or across platforms with different formulas.

### Cited Findings
- "Engagement rate as a single percentage doesn't really tell you much ... it compresses very different behaviors (likes, comments, saves, shares) into one number, with no way to tell how much of it is passive versus higher-quality interaction, and it also doesn't take into account any interactions that happen in DMs and other non-public places"; "two creators can post the exact same engagement rate and it can mean completely different things" — [Influencer Engagement Rate](https://www.modash.io/blog/influencer-engagement-rate)
- "Instagram's hidden likes can throw off the count. Since Instagram lets creators hide their like counts, some engagement tools either miss that data entirely or have to estimate it, which chips away at how accurate the end result is"; "Engagement rate tends to shrink as follower count grows" — [Influencer Engagement Rate](https://www.modash.io/blog/influencer-engagement-rate)
- "Engagement rate is just one piece of the puzzle ... it *doesn't* tell you whether their audience matches yours, whether their personality and values fit your brand, or whether they can actually sell a product" — [Influencer Engagement Rate](https://www.modash.io/blog/influencer-engagement-rate)
- "Because the formulas differ by platform, rates aren't directly comparable across them – Instagram measures against followers, while TikTok and YouTube measure against views" — [Influencer Engagement Rate](https://www.modash.io/blog/influencer-engagement-rate)
- Help FAQ: "**Can engagement rate be manipulated?** Yes, creators can buy fake engagement. That's why Modash also provides audience quality scores and fake follower detection"; "**Should I reject all creators below a certain engagement rate?** Not automatically. Context matters. A mega creator with 1% engagement might still deliver more total reach than a micro creator with 5%" — [Understanding engagement rate](https://help.modash.io/en/articles/6542471-understanding-engagement-rate-and-how-modash-calculates-it)
- "Note that **Discovery ER%** and **Campaign ER%** use different formulas ... so the same creator may show different ER% values in each context" — [Understanding campaign analytics](https://help.modash.io/en/articles/13717084-understanding-campaign-analytics)
- "some values (e.g. engagement rate) may appear outdated compared to others (e.g. follower count), which are refreshed more frequently" (Discovery API guidance for less popular profiles) — [Discovery API: Best Practices & Onboarding](https://help.modash.io/en/articles/10870293-discovery-api-best-practices-onboarding-for-developers)
- Calculator caveats: "Fake followers sit in the denominator without ever engaging, which pulls the rate down. Bought comments do the opposite and push it up artificially"; "Reels, carousels, and static posts all engage differently. A profile average hides that" — [Instagram Engagement Rate Calculator](https://www.modash.io/engagement-rate-calculator)
- API capability note (shows Modash does not treat one aggregation as universal): "Calculation Method: Select median or average (mean) for metrics like ER%, likes, comments, and shares" (July 2025 API changelog entry) — [API Changelog](https://help.modash.io/en/articles/12959883-api-changelog)
- Third-party context only: OWM observes the internal tension between Modash's "good" ranges and its own database medians, cited in section 5 — [OWM](https://www.owm.ai/learn/creator-sourcing/engagement-rate-benchmarks). This is not a Modash self-critique.

### Inferences
- Modash's own documentation supports the conclusion that its Instagram ER is a deliberately simplified, comparable-at-scale proxy — not a full measure of engagement quality, reach, or business impact — and that it should be read alongside audience credibility, comment quality, niche, follower tier, and campaign-specific data.
- The API's selectable median/average calculation method means public calculator/help text may describe the default exposure while the API offers configurability; no primary page clarifies whether the public Instagram ER is always the default median.

### Gaps
- No Modash page directly critiques the choice of a likes-only, follower-based Instagram metric as outdated versus views/reach-based alternatives; it only notes that platform formulas differ and that private interactions are invisible.
- No documentation was found on whether hidden-like accounts are imputed, excluded, or flagged individually in the calculator.
